# AUH → CCJ — Faceless Airport Film
**Zayed International, Terminal A · a6700 + Sony 15mm f/1.4 G · 4:3 Academy · 15–30s**

---

## READ THIS FIRST (2 min)

Abu Dhabi Airports' official position is that photography and videography inside the airport **require prior permission**. Filming in restricted zones (security screening, passport control, customs, immigration, staff areas) is a real offence in the UAE — fines up to AED 5,000 and, in the worst case, a short custodial sentence.

In practice, a passenger holding a small camera in the public departures hall is unremarkable. A passenger **setting up a rig** is not. So the entire plan below is built around this rule:

> **One hand, no setup, keep moving, never point at security.**

Hard boundaries — do not film, not even by accident:

- Security screening lanes and X-ray belts
- Passport control / immigration / e-gates
- Customs
- Any uniformed staff, police, or their faces
- Other passengers' faces (also your faceless concept, so no loss)

If anyone asks you to stop: stop, say yes, offer to delete, don't argue. That's the whole de-escalation script.

**Lowest-risk, highest-reward zones** (in order): the aircraft window at seat 19F → the gate window looking at your aircraft → the central atrium ceiling and *Sana Al Nour* → the piers/travelators → landside kerb and exterior approach.

---

## THE ROOM YOU'RE SHOOTING IN

Terminal A (KPF) is an X-plan building, four piers themed **desert, sea, city, oasis**. Two things matter to you:

1. **The lattice roof** — an interlocking geometric shell inspired by Islamic pattern, undulating like dunes. Massive, symmetrical, backlit by daylight. This is your ceiling shot.
2. **Sana Al Nour** — the central atrium artwork: 1,632 curved translucent glass leaves hanging through the void, channelling light from Departures down to Arrivals. One of the largest covered public artworks in the Middle East. This is your hero object.
3. **The Feature Staircase** — sculptural stair linking the arrivals corridor to the hall, with a panoramic view over the central space. Good for a high looking-down frame.

You have a 22.5mm-equivalent lens. That building was designed to be shot at 22mm. Use it.

## CAMERA SETUP — do this in the taxi, not at the gate

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Codec | XAVC S 4K, 100Mbps+ | Headroom for the 4:3 crop |
| Frame rate | **25p** (base) / **50p** (for slow-mo) | UAE mains is 50 Hz — 25/50 kills LED flicker |
| Shutter | **1/50** | Locked. Do not use 24p/1/48 indoors here |
| Picture Profile | **S-Cinetone** | Graded look straight out of camera, no LUT needed for a 20s IG post |
| Aspect Marker | **4:3, ON** (Menu → Shooting → Marker Display) | You frame 4:3 in-camera, crop in post |
| SteadyShot | **Active** | ~1.1× crop, irrelevant at 15mm, saves handheld shots |
| ISO | Auto, cap **3200** | Terminal A is bright; you'll live at 100–800 |
| AF | AF-C, Wide, **Subject Detection OFF** | It will hunt for faces you don't want |
| Zebras | 100+, ON | Blown windows are the #1 killer here |
| Audio | Record everything | You want ambience even if the shot is unusable |

**The 4:3 workflow:** shoot full-width 3840×2160, crop centre to **2880×2160** (exact 4:3) or **2970×2160** (true 1.375:1 Academy). Deliver at 1440×1080. Posted to the **feed**, 4:3 displays natively at full width. Posted as a **Reel**, it letterboxes inside 9:16 — lean into that, put it on a black or paper-texture background and it reads as projected film.

**The 15mm's superpower:** minimum focus is ~17cm. Put an object 20–25cm from the front element at **f/1.4–f/2** and the entire terminal melts behind it. Nobody's phone can do that. Build the film around this move — it is your signature, and it's the reason to carry the camera at all.

---

## THE MAGIC ARM — be honest about what it's for

A 9.6" magic arm with 1/4" ends is **not** a mounting solution in an airport. Anything that looks like a rigged tripod invites a conversation. Use it as:

- **A grip extension.** Screw it into the camera's base, hold the far end. You now have a 25cm boom — floor-level sweeps without lying down, and rise/fall moves that read like a tiny jib.
- **A low-angle handle.** Camera 15cm off the terrazzo, pointed up at the lattice ceiling, wheels rolling past. Two seconds, then it's back in the bag.
- **A trolley dolly** *(only if you have a super clamp)*. Clamp to your cabin trolley handle, camera facing forward, walk the pier. It's the smoothest tracking shot you'll get all day. It also draws the most attention — do it once, in a quiet stretch of a pier, and be finished inside 30 seconds.

If you don't have a clamp: rest the camera flat on the trolley's top face, hold it with one hand, and walk. 80% of the shot, 5% of the attention.

## CONCEPT 1 — "CARRY-ON"
*Objects and thresholds. The whole film happens below shoulder height.*

**Logline:** A departure told entirely through the things you're holding. No face, no body above the chest.
**Runtime:** 22s · 12 shots · cut on a 100 BPM beat (~0.6s per bar-half)
**Vibe:** warm, tactile, quiet — reads as film stock, not travel vlog.

| # | Time | Shot | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | Sling bag zip closing, ECU | Camera 20cm away, **f/1.4**. Terminal lattice a soft blur behind. Hold 1.5s |
| 2 | 0:01.5 | Shoes + trolley wheels crossing frame | Magic arm, camera 15cm off floor, **static**. Let feet enter and exit. f/4 |
| 3 | 0:02.5 | Ceiling — the lattice, dead symmetrical | Camera flat on your palm, pointed straight up, walk slowly. f/5.6. **Centre the vanishing point** |
| 4 | 0:04 | Boarding pass held out, terminal behind | 25cm from lens, f/1.4, focus on the pass. Tilt it so light rakes across |
| 5 | 0:06 | Travelator — handrail rushing past | Camera low on the rail, angled down its length. Let the rubber blur. 50p → 50% speed |
| 6 | 0:08 | *Sana Al Nour* — slow tilt up through the glass leaves | Two-hand, elbows in, tilt over 3s. f/2.8. This is your hero shot, give it room |
| 7 | 0:11 | Coffee cup / water bottle set down, ECU | Your $2–5 purchase, earning its keep. f/1.4, condensation and light |
| 8 | 0:12.5 | Departure board — CCJ rolling into frame | f/2.8, focus on your line only, everything else soft |
| 9 | 0:14 | Gate window: silhouette of the A321, you not in it | Lens **against the glass** to kill reflections. Expose for the sky, let the interior go black |
| 10 | 0:16 | Jet bridge — walking, camera at hip pointed forward | Active SteadyShot on. The tunnel does the composition for you |
| 11 | 0:18 | Seat 19F: seatbelt buckle clicking, ECU | f/1.4, 20cm. Window light from the right |
| 12 | 0:20 | Window — wing, then the ground drops away | Hold to the end. Let the shot outlive the music |

**Ending card (optional):** `AUH → CCJ` in a thin mono type, 1s, over the last frame.

## CONCEPT 2 — "SANA AL NOUR"
*No story. Pure architecture, light and pattern. The safest film to shoot and the most likely to travel.*

**Logline:** A building made of 1,632 pieces of glass, cut like a piece of music.
**Runtime:** 18s · 9 shots · match-cut on shape
**Vibe:** cool, graphic, near-monochrome with one warm accent.

The rule for this one: **every shot is either dead-centre symmetrical or a hard diagonal.** Nothing in between. That single constraint is what makes it look designed.

| # | Time | Shot | How |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0:00 | Black → the lattice roof fades in, symmetrical | Static 2s. Find a floor tile, stand on it, point straight up |
| 2 | 0:02 | Slow push into a single glass leaf of *Sana Al Nour* | Walk forward 1.5m over 2.5s. f/2. Let one leaf fill frame |
| 3 | 0:04.5 | Cut to a repeating column line — hard diagonal | f/5.6. Put the vanishing point in a corner, not the centre |
| 4 | 0:06 | Light through the glass onto the floor | Camera 20cm from the terrazzo, f/1.4, focus on the caustic |
| 5 | 0:08 | Feature Staircase from above, looking down the void | Symmetrical. People become moving dots — no faces, all scale |
| 6 | 0:10 | Escalator handrail, extreme diagonal, motion blur | 50p, then 40% speed. f/4 |
| 7 | 0:12 | Reflection of the roof in polished floor / dark glass | Turn the camera upside down. Nobody will know until the end |
| 8 | 0:14 | The **one warm shot**: sunlight hitting a wall, orange | Everything before this was blue. This is the payoff |
| 9 | 0:16 | Pull back to the widest symmetrical frame you can find | 2s hold, then cut to black on the beat |

**Grade:** push blues into the shadows, hold skin-free neutrals, single warm highlight in shot 8. Slight halation. Grain at 4–6%.

**Runtime:** 15s · 10 shots · hard cuts, 1 number per beat

## BONUS — "THE $3 FILM"
If you want something with a wink: buy one small thing (water, a chocolate bar, a coffee). Make **that object** the protagonist. It appears in every shot — held against the lattice ceiling, sitting on the gate seat, on the tray table, silhouetted against the window at 35,000 ft. Faceless, cheap, funny, and it gives an otherwise abstract film a spine. 8 shots, 15 seconds. The joke lands in the caption.

## SEAT 19F — THE FLIGHT PLAN

**Check first: jet bridge or bus gate?** Low-cost carriers at Terminal A frequently board from a **remote stand**. If yours does, you get the best shot of the day for free — walking across the apron toward a lit A321, faceless, low angle, plane filling frame. It is also the most sensitive place you'll stand all trip. If it happens: one shot, handheld, camera down between frames, thirty seconds, done. If a jet bridge instead, use the tunnel — the shape does the composing for you.

**What 19F gives you.** Right-hand window, emergency exit row on the A321 — extra legroom means room to compose, and you're at or just ahead of the wing leading edge. Wing in frame is free production value.

**What the exit row takes away.** Crew are strict: nothing on the floor, nothing loose, bag in the overhead for taxi, takeoff and landing. **Assume you cannot hold the camera during takeoff or landing.** Plan for your window footage to come from cruise and descent. If the crew are relaxed and you ask nicely, you may get the climb — ask, don't assume.

**Shooting through the window:**

- **Shoot wide open, f/1.4, focus at infinity.** The scratches and haze in the perspex sit far outside the depth of field and vanish completely. This is the single best trick you have.
- Get the front element as close to the glass as you can **without touching it** — 2–3cm. Kills reflections.
- **No polariser.** Aircraft windows are stress-birefringent; you'll get rainbow banding.
- Kill your overhead light, and if reflections persist, drape a dark shirt over the camera and the window edge.
- Wipe the inner pane with a dry cloth. Don't touch the outer.

**What's out the right side, AUH → CCJ:** you climb out over the UAE and Oman — expect desert, salt flats and the edge of the sand sea on the right early on, then open Arabian Sea for most of the cruise. On descent into Kozhikode you get the Malabar coast: palm green, red earth, backwaters, paddy. **Have the camera out 25 minutes before landing** — the coast comes up fast and then you're told to stow it.

**Light:** if it's an evening departure, the sun sets behind your right shoulder — 19F gets the glow. Morning departure and you're on the shade side, which is actually better for clean detail without flare.

## THE 60-MINUTE AIRSIDE ROUTE

You have 1–2 hours. Don't wander. Do this:

1. **Minutes 0–10** — Clear security, walk to your gate first, confirm it, then walk back. Now you know the geography and you're not shooting under time pressure.
2. **Minutes 10–25** — Central atrium. *Sana Al Nour* and the lattice roof. This is 40% of your film. Get the ceiling, the leaves, the light on the floor, the staircase looking down.
3. **Minutes 25–40** — Walk your pier. Travelator handrail, column rhythm, signage diagonals, the trolley move if you're doing it.
4. **Minutes 40–50** — Buy the $3 object. Shoot it. Shoot the boarding pass and the departure board.
5. **Minutes 50–60** — At the gate: window shot of the aircraft, then **record 60 seconds of clean ambience** — no shot, just audio. Announcements, wheels, the hum. This is your sound bed and it's what will make the edit feel expensive.
6. **Onboard** — buckle, seat number, safety card, window.

**Non-negotiable minimum — if everything goes wrong, get these six:**
lattice ceiling (symmetrical) · *Sana Al Nour* tilt · close-focus f/1.4 object with terminal behind · low-angle wheels · aircraft through gate glass · window at cruise.

## POST

- **Crop** 3840×2160 → 2880×2160 (4:3) or 2970×2160 (1.375 Academy). Export 1440×1080.
- **Cut to the ambience first**, then lay music under it. Airport sound under music is the difference between a travel clip and a film.
- **Sound punctuation:** zip, seatbelt click, boarding chime, engine spool. One per section.
- **Grade:** S-Cinetone needs almost nothing — lift the black point a touch for a film toe, desaturate greens, warm the highlights. 4–6% grain. Slight halation on the window shots.
- **Length discipline:** if it's 30s, it's too long. Cut to 22s. Then cut to 18s.
- **First frame matters more than the film.** Whatever is most graphic and most symmetrical goes first — that's what stops the scroll.
- **Caption:** don't explain the film. `AUH → CCJ.` is enough.
